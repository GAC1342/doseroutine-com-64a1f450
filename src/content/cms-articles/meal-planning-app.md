## MyFitnessPal: Best for Large-Database Calorie Tracking

If calorie counting is your primary focus, this is likely the name you already know. With a food database of [over 20.5 million items](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.myfitnesspal.android&hl=en_US), MyFitnessPal holds the largest nutritional database available in any consumer app today. That scale covers everything from restaurant menu items and packaged goods to user-contributed entries spanning 30-plus cuisines, making it genuinely hard to stump when you're logging a meal.

The 2026 feature rollout has brought MyFitnessPal meaningfully closer to the AI-first standard the market now expects. The new Meal Scan feature uses your camera to recognize food on a plate and log calories, macros, and portions automatically. Voice Log lets you speak your meals instead of searching and tapping. Both are Premium-tier features, which matters when you consider that Premium runs [$19.99 per month or $79.99 per year](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/myfitnesspal-calorie-counter/id341232718). The free version still covers basic food and calorie logging, but the removal of barcode scanning from the free tier a few years back is worth knowing upfront if you're budget-conscious.

On the fundamentals, MyFitnessPal tracks calories, protein, carbs, fat, sodium, sugar, and fiber with solid reliability. It connects with 40-plus fitness apps and devices including Apple Health, Garmin, and Fitbit, so your exercise data flows in without manual entry. For users whose main goal is energy balance and weight management, that foundation is genuinely useful.

Where it falls short is everything beyond food. There is no supplement or peptide dose logging, no vial tracking, no hormone panel recording, and no structured progressive workout programming built in. If your health routine extends beyond calories and macros, you will need additional tools to cover the rest.

**Best for:** users who want deep, database-backed calorie and macro tracking and are comfortable managing every other aspect of their health protocol in a separate app.

## FAQs

**What should a meal planning app actually do?**
The useful ones handle recipe or meal scheduling, an accurate food database, portion and macro maths, a grocery list, and a fast way to log what you really ate.

**Are free meal planning apps good enough?**
For basic weekly menus, often yes. Limits usually show up in database accuracy, barcode coverage, custom macro targets and exporting your own data.

**How accurate are app calorie counts?**
Accuracy depends on the database entry, not the app's design. Verified sources such as USDA FoodData Central and label-backed barcode records are far more reliable than user-submitted entries.

**Can a meal planner work alongside supplement or peptide timing?**
Yes, and that is where most standalone planners stop. Pairing meals with dose timing matters for compounds affected by food, minerals or fasting windows.
