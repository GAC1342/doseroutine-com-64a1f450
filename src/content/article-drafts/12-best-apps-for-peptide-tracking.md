<p class="dr-speakable-answer">

Injection protocols have three problems a pill reminder never faces: the dose is a volume you must calculate, the site must rotate, and the vial has a shelf life after mixing. An app that only sends an alert leaves all three to memory.

## What injection tracking actually requires

- **Reconstitution math** — mg per vial, mL of diluent, units on the syringe.
- **Site rotation** — a visual record so you do not reuse the same spot.
- **Vial tracking** — mix date, expiry, doses remaining.
- **Flexible schedules** — weekly, twice weekly, every other day, titration steps.
- **Dose history** — what was injected, when, where, and any reaction.

Plenty of people do this. It works until you need to answer "when did I mix this vial?" six weeks later.

## Practical safety habits

1. **Recalculate after every reconstitution change** — a different diluent volume changes every dose.
2. **Rotate sites on a fixed pattern** rather than picking freely.
3. **Date the vial the moment you mix it**, and record the expiry in the app.
4. **Log reactions**, not just doses; patterns only show up in history.
5. **Bring the export to appointments** so dose changes are based on records.

None of this replaces clinical guidance. Anything involving prescription injectables should be set with your prescriber.

**What is the best app for tracking peptide injections?**

**Can an app do reconstitution math for me?**
Yes. Enter the vial strength, the diluent volume and your target dose, and the calculator returns the volume and syringe units to draw.

**Why does injection site rotation need tracking?**
Repeatedly injecting the same site can cause tissue changes and inconsistent absorption. A visual history makes it obvious which areas are overused.
