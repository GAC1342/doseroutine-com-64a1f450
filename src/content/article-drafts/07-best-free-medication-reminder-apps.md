# Best Free Medication Reminder Apps (No Subscription Needed)

<p class="dr-speakable-answer">
The best free medication reminder apps in 2026 are DoseRoutine (free to start, no card, full reminder scheduling), Apple Health Medications and Google-side equivalents (free but iPhone- or Android-only), Medisafe (free with ads), and MyTherapy (free with adherence reports). Reminders themselves are almost always free — what you pay for is history, exports and sharing.
</p>

"Free" means different things in this category. Some apps give you unlimited reminders and charge for history. Some show ads. Some cap how many medications you can add. Here is what each free tier actually covers.

## What is normally free

- Adding medications and setting daily or twice-daily reminders.
- Marking a dose taken or skipped.
- A few weeks of recent history.

## What is normally paid

- Long-term history and adherence trends.
- CSV or PDF export for a doctor's visit.
- Family or caregiver sharing.
- Advanced schedules: cycles, tapers, injection tracking.

## 1. DoseRoutine — free to start, no card

You can build a full schedule — prescriptions, supplements and injections — log doses and see recent adherence without paying or entering a card. Paid unlocks deeper history, exports and advanced protocol tools. No ads at any tier.

See the full ranking in [best medication reminder apps](/articles/best-medication-reminder-apps), or open the [manual](/manual) for setup.

## 2. Apple Health Medications — free, iPhone only

No cost, no install, no ads. Thin logging and no supplement or injection support, and nothing on Android. More detail in [best medication reminder apps for iPhone](/articles/best-medication-reminder-apps-iphone).

## 3. Medisafe — free with ads

Generous free tier with caregiver alerts, but interstitial ads and repeated upgrade prompts. Fine if you can ignore them.

## 4. MyTherapy — free with reports

Reminders, measurements and printable adherence reports at no cost. Weak on cycling or injection protocols.

## 5. Your phone's built-in alarm — free, but limited

An alarm rings; it does not record whether you took anything. If you want to answer "did I take it?" an hour later, you need logging. That difference is covered in [pill reminder apps vs protocol trackers](/articles/pill-reminder-app).

## How to choose a free app you will not outgrow

Ask three questions before you commit:

1. **Will my routine grow?** Adding a supplement or a weekly injection later breaks simple apps.
2. **Do I need my history?** If you will show a clinician, check export is available.
3. **Is anyone else involved?** Caregiver sharing is the most commonly paywalled feature.

If your routine already includes supplements, start with [best apps for tracking supplements and stacks](/articles/best-apps-for-tracking-supplements) instead. Choosing for an older parent? Free options are compared in [best pill reminder apps for seniors](/articles/best-pill-reminder-apps-for-seniors), and Android free tiers in [best medication reminder apps for Android](/articles/best-medication-reminder-apps-android) — moving your data later is the annoying part.

## FAQs

**Is there a genuinely free medication reminder app?**
Yes. DoseRoutine is free to start with no card and no ads, and Apple Health Medications is free on iPhone. Both cover reminders and basic logging.

**What do paid medication apps add?**
Usually long-term history, exports for clinicians, family sharing, and advanced schedules such as cycles, tapers and injection site rotation.

**Do free apps sell my health data?**
Some ad-supported apps monetize usage data. Read the privacy policy and prefer apps that state health data is not sold; DoseRoutine does not sell health data.

**Do free reminders still work offline?**
Yes. Reminders are scheduled on the device, so alerts fire without a connection. Sync and backups catch up when you are online again.
